02 · What's real and what's ours

Every number carries a tag.

So nobody mistakes a guess for a spec. Every parameter in the Lab shows its tag and its source.

REPORTED

Said publicly at the 2017 or 2026 reveal, or in Tesla's wing patent. Thruster count, tank size, "up to 2 g" of suction, 10,000 N·m at the wheels, 700 kg of wing downforce.

ESTIMATE

Measured from the reveal photos or worked out from reference data: the body (4.52 × 2.00 × 1.19 m), mass, tyre grip, drag, and nitrogen density from NIST tables.

HYPOTHETICAL

Our design, so the systems can run at all: where each nozzle sits, its regulator and expansion ratio, the skirts, and the flight controller.

03 · Same physics, smaller hardware

What the club would build next.

Each system scales down to a robot we can afford. We'd design it in this simulator before buying a single part.

Wall-climbing robotfrom the suction fans

Suction = pressure drop × sealed area, against leakage under the skirt. One ducted fan, a foam skirt and a pressure sensor. Inspects walls, tanks and bridges.

Thruster pointing rigfrom the cold-gas nozzles

Thrust = Cf × chamber pressure × throat area. Low-pressure air, solenoid valves and an IMU on a single bearing: how satellites aim.

Self-levelling dronefrom the flight controller

The Lab's step tests tune the same PD loop and thrust mixer. A quadcopter frame running our own controller instead of a stock one.

Traction-control RC carfrom the tyre model

Grip peaks near 10 % slip, then falls away. Wheel encoders and torque limiting in firmware keep it at the peak, like the car's traction control.

Safety: nothing we build gets near 690 bar. Club builds run a few bar from bike pumps or CO₂ cartridges, under supervision.
