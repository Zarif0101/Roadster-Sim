Roadster Lab

On 15 October Tesla drove a Roadster up a curved ramp, hung it upside down, dropped it and flipped it back onto its wheels. We rebuilt that stunt in a physics engine to see what it actually takes: how much thrust, how much air, how much suction.

What's real and what's ours

Every number in the simulator carries one of three tags, so nobody mistakes a guess for a spec.

REPORTED

Said publicly at the 2017 or 2026 reveal. Thruster count, tank size, "up to 2 g" of fan suction, 10,000 N·m of wheel torque.

ESTIMATE

Worked out from those numbers or from similar cars. Mass, tyre grip, drag, how many kilograms of air fit in the tank.

HYPOTHETICAL

Our design, so the systems can run at all. Where each thruster sits, how hard it pushes, and the flight controller.

Limits. This is a model, not the car. It passes our own consistency checks (free fall, torque, air use, frame-rate independence) but nothing here was measured on a real Roadster. Tyres, gas flow and fans are simplified, and the Data panel lists exactly how.

Same physics, smaller hardware

What we'd build next

Each system in the car scales down to a club project. We'd design and test each one in this simulator before buying a single part.

Wall-climbing robot

From the suction fans.

Physics
Suction = pressure drop × sealed area. It has to beat weight ÷ grip to stay up.
Build
Small chassis, one ducted fan, foam skirt, pressure sensor.
Why
Inspecting walls, tanks and bridges.

Thruster pointing rig

From the cold-gas thrusters.

Physics
Thrust = air flow × exhaust speed. Off-centre thrust spins the rig: torque = r × F.
Build
Low-pressure air bottle, solenoid valves, IMU, rig on a single bearing.
Why
It's how satellites and landers aim.

Self-levelling drone

From the flight controller.

Physics
PD control plus a thrust mixer, the same code the hover mode runs.
Build
Quadcopter frame running our own controller instead of a stock one.
Why
Every flying or balancing robot needs it.

Traction-control RC car

From the tyre model.

Physics
Grip is capped at μ × load. Push past it and the tyre slides.
Build
1:10 car, wheel encoders, torque limiting in firmware.
Why
Faster laps, fewer crashes, on any surface.

Safety: nothing we build gets near 690 bar. Club builds would run a few bar from bike pumps or CO₂ cartridges, under supervision.
